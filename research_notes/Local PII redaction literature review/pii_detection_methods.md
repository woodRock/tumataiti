## 1. Evolution of de-identification: rules, CRF/BiLSTM-CRF, clinical de-id, transformers

### Takeaway
Clinical de-identification moved from lexicon/regex systems (Scrub 1996, PhysioNet `deid` 2008) to feature-rich CRFs combined with rules (the i2b2 2006/2014 winners), then to BiLSTM-CRF (NeuroNER, about 97.9 F1 on i2b2 2014) and pretrained transformers. Strong systems have nearly always been hybrids. On curated clinical corpora, entity F1 reached the mid-to-high 90s years ago. The hard parts that remain are recall on rare categories, cross-institution transfer and domain shift. Pure-rule systems survive where recall is mandated: Philter is recall-oriented, at the cost of precision.

### Inferences
- Across the i2b2 lineage the winning recipe is consistently "ML for open-class entities (names, locations, organisations) + regex/dictionaries for closed-format identifiers". Tūmataiti's architecture follows this proven pattern. Its novelty lies in country-specific checksum validators (IRD, NHI) and leakage-oriented evaluation, not in the hybrid idea itself.
- Clinical results in the high 90s were reached on in-domain test sets, while N-GRID 2016 showed large drops under note-type shift. Tūmataiti should therefore report results on its NZ set separately from PII-Masking-300k rather than pooling them.

### Gaps
- Exact per-system numbers for the Philter and PhysioNet `deid` papers were not re-fetched this session; verify against the DOIs.
- No NZ- or Australasia-specific clinical de-id corpus or study was found in this pass. A targeted search (e.g., NZ Health Information Privacy Code, NHI de-identification) would be needed.

## 2. Hybrid rule + ML systems, checksum validation, and Presidio

### Takeaway
Hybrids combine rules and models in four main ways: (a) category routing (rules own structured IDs, the model owns names); (b) union with conflict resolution by priority or confidence; (c) model-proposes / rule-validates (checksums cut regex false positives); (d) rule-proposes / LLM-disambiguates (RECAP 2025). Presidio is the reference open-source architecture of type (b)+(c): regex recognizers with checksum `validate` hooks, context-word score boosting and a spaCy/Stanza/transformers NER engine. Its 2026 entity list covers Australia (ABN, ACN, TFN, Medicare, all checksum-validated) but **no New Zealand identifiers**. Independent evaluations repeatedly find Presidio high-recall/low-precision on dates and names but reliable on checksum-backed IDs.

- **[V] Rajgarhia, H., Gupta, S., Shaik, A., Kumar, G.P., Santhoshraj, Y., Nishitha, S.N.T., Mukherji, A. (2025). - **[K] Yang & Garibaldi (2015) and Liu et al. (2017)** (Section 1) are peer-reviewed examples of rules for format-based PHI and of category-wise ensemble selection. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbi.2015.06.015); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbi.2017.05.023)

### Inferences
- **Novelty claim for Tūmataiti:** Presidio's absence of NZ recognisers (IRD, NHI, NZ bank account, NZ driver licence, NZ passport) is a concrete, citable gap. Checksum validation is established practice (Presidio does it for ~30 identifiers in other countries), so the contribution is the NZ coverage plus a portable Rust/WASM implementation, not the checksum idea itself.
- Evidence across sources suggests checksums mainly buy **precision** on numeric strings: Presidio's dates and generic numbers are the weak points, while its checksum entities are strong. Tūmataiti could report rule-layer precision with and without validation as an ablation.
- How rules and model outputs are merged is under-reported in the literature. Most papers use simple union or category routing. A short, explicit description of Tūmataiti's merge policy (priority, overlap resolution) would help.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed, Presidio-authored evaluation exists. All Presidio numbers come from third-party preprints with different configurations (spaCy model size, thresholds, matching rules), so they are not comparable with one another.
- PIIBench and MathEd-PII authors and full numbers were not verified beyond search snippets.

## 3. Recent general-purpose PII models: OpenAI Privacy Filter, GLiNER-PII, ai4privacy, LLMs

### Takeaway
OpenAI Privacy Filter (OPF; Apache-2.0, model card 22 Apr 2026, arXiv 18 Aug 2026) is a 1.5B-total / ~50M-active-parameter MoE converted from an autoregressive LM into a bidirectional banded-attention token classifier. It predicts BIOES over 8 categories with constrained Viterbi decoding and tunable operating points. It reports token F1 0.960 (0.974 after label correction) on PII-Masking-300k, but independent work shows collapse on non-Latin scripts and weak name and address F1 outside synthetic data. GLiNER-family PII models are compact span-extractors with label-agnostic prompts, and their self-reported results are hard to compare. Fine-tuned encoders generally beat zero-shot LLMs on span F1, but GPT-4o-class models lead in some professional domains (medical/legal) and hybrids beat both.

### Cited Findings
**OpenAI Privacy Filter**
- **[V] de Bourcy, C., Ghalebikesabi, S., Schwarzschild, A., Gorbachev, A., Maruseac, M., Chu, A., Kyrylov, V., Mu, T., Bennett, A., Nguyen, A., Meehan, C., Lee, J.G., Bauer, S., Nguyen, H., Eckhardt, R., Liu, Y., Oxborough, C., Rougeth, M., Chedid, O., Costa, C., Parikh, Y., Li, Y., Song, C., Thakkar, O., Monaco, V. (2026). "Model Card for OpenAI Privacy Filter." arXiv:2608.18274 (technical report; model card PDF dated April 22, 2026).** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18274); [PDF](https://cdn.openai.com/pdf/c66281ed-b638-456a-8ce1-97e9f5264a90/OpenAI-Privacy-Filter-Model-Card.pdf); [HF card](https://huggingface.co/openai/privacy-filter); [GitHub](https://github.com/openai/privacy-filter)
  - *Architecture:* gpt-oss-like but smaller. 8 pre-norm blocks, d_model 640, GQA (14 query / 2 KV heads) with RoPE, sparse MoE FFN (128 experts, top-4). Bidirectional banded attention with band 128 (effective window 257). 128k context. 1.5B total and ~50M active parameters (HF metadata says "1B", an unreconciled discrepancy). — [HF card](https://huggingface.co/openai/privacy-filter)
  - *Labels/decoding:* `account_number, private_address, private_email, private_person, private_phone, private_url, private_date, secret` × BIOES + O = 33 classes. Constrained linear-chain Viterbi with six transition-bias parameters act as precision/recall operating-point controls. Operating points were calibrated on span-level P/R/F1/F2. — [HF card](https://huggingface.co/openai/privacy-filter); [PDF](https://cdn.openai.com/pdf/c66281ed-b638-456a-8ce1-97e9f5264a90/OpenAI-Privacy-Filter-Model-Card.pdf)
  - *Training data:* "a mix of publicly available data and internally generated synthetic datasets". Missing ground truth was annotated by a GPT-5-family model under a 2×2 protocol (JSON-offsets vs inline tags × medium/high reasoning). Synthetic data used format-matching augmentation plus automated QC. — [PDF](https://cdn.openai.com/pdf/c66281ed-b638-456a-8ce1-97e9f5264a90/OpenAI-Privacy-Filter-Model-Card.pdf)
  - *PII-Masking-300k results (test split only; not trained on its train split; labels remapped to 8 categories, e.g. givenname/lastname/title/username → private_person):* baseline token P/R/F1 0.940/0.980/0.960, span F1 0.926. Corrected token P/R/F1 0.968/0.981/0.974, span F1 0.942. CredData `secret`: token F1 0.842, span F1 0.617. Span scores sit below token scores because of boundary disagreements. — [PDF](https://cdn.openai.com/pdf/c66281ed-b638-456a-8ce1-97e9f5264a90/OpenAI-Privacy-Filter-Model-Card.pdf)
  - *Fine-tuning efficiency on SPY:* zero-shot token F1 0.545 (P 0.414, R 0.795). With 1% of train: 0.879. With 10%: 0.962. With 50%: 0.983. — [PDF](https://cdn.openai.com/pdf/c66281ed-b638-456a-8ce1-97e9f5264a90/OpenAI-Privacy-Filter-Model-Card.pdf)
  - *Stated limitations:* it is a redaction/data-minimisation aid, "not an anonymization, compliance, or safety guarantee". The label policy is static. It misses "uncommon or regional names, initials", regional naming conventions and novel credential formats. It over-redacts public entities and placeholders. Boundaries fragment on long or messy text. It is primarily English. Names "underrepresented in training data" are more likely to be missed. — [HF card](https://huggingface.co/openai/privacy-filter); [PDF](https://cdn.openai.com/pdf/c66281ed-b638-456a-8ce1-97e9f5264a90/OpenAI-Privacy-Filter-Model-Card.pdf)
- **[V] Uppala, R. (2026). "OpenAI Privacy Filter: A Cross-Lingual, Cross-Domain PII Evaluation Across 32 Benchmarks." arXiv:2608.02616 (preprint; v1 23 May 2026, v2 6 Aug 2026; the v1 HTML title says "42 benchmarks").** Covers 14 languages and 5 domains. Zero-shot F1 was 0.855 on AI4Privacy, above Presidio and XLM-R-large-NER, but 0.464 on SPY medical and 0.04–0.40 on general NER sets. - **[V] Singh, D., Narayanan, S. (2025). "Unmasking the Reality of PII Masking Models: Performance Gaps and the Call for Accountability." arXiv:2504.12308 (preprint).** Audits the popular HF models Piiranha (>300k downloads) and StarPII (>580k) on a 17k-sentence semi-synthetic set (16 PII types; India/UK/US rules). Six dimensions: basic, contextual disambiguation, noisy text, novel entities, cross-lingual, adversarial. It finds significant failures and calls for contextual disclosure in model cards. A secondary source says the same work audited the ai4privacy 200k labels (225 unique tags, redundancy, "heuristic leakage", variants such as [TEL] vs [TEL_B]); this was **not** visible on the abstract page and needs checking in the PDF. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.12308)
- **[V, via search snippet] "A Comparative Study of Light-weight Language Models for PII Masking and their Deployment for Real Conversational Texts." arXiv:2512.18608 (preprint, Dec 2025).** Authors and results not verified. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.18608)

**LLM-based detection vs fine-tuned encoders**
- **[V, via search snippet] CAPID: "Context-Aware PII Detection for Question-Answering Systems." arXiv:2602.10074 (preprint, 2026).** A fine-tuned Llama-3.1-8B raised span F1 from 0.48 to 0.96. GPT-4.1-mini reached span recall 0.94 but did not match the fine-tuned models. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10074)
- **[V, via search snippet] PII-TRACE: "A Benchmark for Context-Aware PII Detection." arXiv:2609.22200 (preprint, 2026).** A compact 0.6B detector is competitive with zero-shot frontier LLMs in dialogue and single-record settings. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22200)
- **[V] Brynjólfsson, S., Jayakrishnan, S., Sali, E., Purwar, D., Aggarwal, M. (2026). "RedactionBench." arXiv:2606.18782 (preprint).** 200 manually annotated, mostly real-seeded documents across 11 domains, grounded in contextual integrity. 35 systems evaluated (NER models, small LMs, frontier LLMs with agentic tools). Its character-level R-Score ignores masking-style differences. Human agreement was 89.4% on mandatory redactions but only 47.7% on contextual ones. "Contextual redaction remains an unsolved problem." — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18782)
- **[V, via search snippet] PII-Bench: "Evaluating Query-Aware Privacy Protection Systems." arXiv:2502.18545 (preprint, 2025).** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.18545)
- **[K] Staab, R., Vero, M., Balunović, M., Vechev, M. (2024). "Beyond Memorization: Violating Privacy via Inference with Large Language Models." *ICLR 2024*. arXiv:2310.07298.** LLMs infer personal attributes (location, income, age) from text with no explicit PII spans. Span redaction does not equal anonymisation, a caveat for any leakage metric defined over gold spans. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.07298)

### Inferences
- OPF's own failure-mode list ("regional names", underrepresented naming conventions) plus Uppala's name F1 of 0.40 directly motivates an NZ-specific student model: te reo Māori and Pasifika names are a plausible blind spot. This is an inference; no source tests Māori names.
- OPF's corrected-vs-baseline gap (0.960 → 0.974) shows that on PII-Masking-300k, a sizeable share of apparent false positives are missing gold labels. If Tūmataiti distils from OPF and scores on raw 300k labels, its precision will be underestimated. Its recall/leakage number is less affected, but it is still affected by OPF's mapping choices.
- The OPF taxonomy has no health, affiliation or government-ID-specific category: `account_number` absorbs IDs. Tūmataiti's health/affiliation labels and IRD/NHI rules therefore extend beyond the teacher's label space, and the teacher cannot supervise those classes.
- Uppala's finding that about 100–1,000 labelled examples let XLM-R beat zero-shot OPF supports a small, high-quality NZ annotated set as a cost-effective investment.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed head-to-head of OPF vs GLiNER-PII vs Presidio on a common benchmark with common metrics was found. All comparisons are preprints or vendor material.
- ai4privacy's own trained models (e.g., DeBERTa-based PII maskers) were not individually verified this session.
- The full text of CAPID, PII-TRACE and PII-Bench (authors, metric definitions) was not read.

## 4. Benchmarks, datasets and evaluation methodology

### Takeaway
PII-Masking-300k is large, multilingual and synthetic. Its maintainers self-report about 98.3% token-label accuracy on a 216-entry sample, and OpenAI's audit found about 10k missing-label spans in 8k test examples. Scores on it are therefore partly a function of label noise. TAB (Pilán et al. 2022) is the main real-text benchmark with re-identification-oriented metrics: entity-level recall split by direct and quasi identifiers, plus information-weighted precision. Clinical corpora (i2b2 2006/2014, N-GRID 2016) remain the peer-reviewed standard but are access-controlled. Metric choice (token vs span, strict vs partial, micro vs per-type) can swing results substantially, and aggregate F1 hides deployment-critical recall failures.

### Cited Findings
**Datasets**
- **[V] ai4privacy (2024). PII-Masking-300k (Hugging Face dataset).** Synthetic multilingual PII text with span labels (e.g., givenname1/2, lastname1–3, title, username, ID types). The README estimates about 98.3% token-label accuracy from a QA sample of 216 entries. Known errors concentrate on coarse types (country, time, titles such as "Dr."). — [HF README](https://huggingface.co/datasets/ai4privacy/pii-masking-300k/blob/main/README.md)
- **[V] OPF model card label correction for PII-Masking-300k.** Step 1: a rule-based pass removed dataset false positives: the same substring under conflicting categories, and placeholders such as "no disponible" or "Unknown". Step 2: model-predicted spans absent from gold were adjudicated by GPT-5.4 (high reasoning). About 10k spans in 8k examples were marked missing labels, with 100/100 agreement on a human-validated sample. Adjudicating gold spans the model missed reached only 85% human agreement, so those were not corrected. This is the most detailed public evidence of 300k label problems; no corrected dataset release was found. — [PDF](https://cdn.openai.com/pdf/c66281ed-b638-456a-8ce1-97e9f5264a90/OpenAI-Privacy-Filter-Model-Card.pdf)
- **[K] Pilán, I., Lison, P., Øvrelid, L., Papadopoulou, A., Sánchez, D., Batet, M. (2022). "The Text Anonymization Benchmark (TAB): A Dedicated Corpus and Evaluation Framework for Text Anonymization." *Computational Linguistics* 48(4):1053–1101. DOI 10.1162/coli_a_00458. arXiv:2202.00443.** 1,268 English ECHR court cases annotated for semantic category, identifier type (direct vs quasi-identifier), confidential attributes and co-reference, with multiple annotators. It proposes **entity-level recall** (an entity counts as protected only if *all* its mentions are masked; computed separately for direct and quasi identifiers), **token-level recall**, and **weighted precision** (masked tokens weighted by information content, estimated with a BERT LM). Baselines (spaCy/Presidio-style NER, fine-tuned Longformer) show that NER categories poorly match what needs masking. The entity-level "any mention leaks = failure" metric is the closest published analogue to Tūmataiti's document leakage rate. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1162/coli_a_00458); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.00443)
- **[K] i2b2 2006 / 2014 and CEGS N-GRID 2016** (Section 1). Access via n2c2/DBMI Data Portal under DUA. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbi.2015.07.020)
- **[K] Tjong Kim Sang, E.F., De Meulder, F. (2003). "Introduction to the CoNLL-2003 Shared Task: Language-Independent Named Entity Recognition." *CoNLL 2003*, 142–147.** PER/LOC/ORG/MISC NER benchmark. Its PER class is a proxy for name detection only. — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/W03-0419/)
- **[K] Pradhan, S., Moschitti, A., Xue, N., Ng, H.T., Björkelund, A., Uryupina, O., Zhang, Y., Zhong, Z. (2013). "Towards Robust Linguistic Analysis using OntoNotes." *CoNLL 2013*, 143–152.** OntoNotes 5.0 with 18 NER types; it is the training source for spaCy's English NER and hence for Presidio's PERSON/LOC/DATE detection. — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/W13-3516/)
- **[K] Marimon, M., Gonzalez-Agirre, A., Intxaurrondo, A., Rodríguez, H., Lopez Martin, J.A., Villegas, M., Krallinger, M. (2019). "Automatic De-identification of Medical Texts in Spanish: the MEDDOCAN Track, Corpus, Guidelines, Methods and Evaluation of Results." *IberLEF 2019*, CEUR-WS Vol. 2421, 618–638.** A non-English synthetic clinical de-id benchmark. Best systems reached above 0.96 F1. — [CEUR-WS](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-2421/MEDDOCAN_overview.pdf)
- **[V] SPY** (Savkin et al. 2025; Section 2): legal and medical synthetic data with Faker-filled entities. — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2025.naacl-srw.23)
- **[V] RedactionBench** (2026; Section 3): real-seeded, contextual-integrity labels and character-level R-Score. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18782)
- **[V, via snippet] PIIBench** (arXiv:2604.15776), a multi-source unified corpus; **Mind the Gap** OOD stress set (arXiv:2609.03464). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.15776); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03464)

**Metric methodology**
- **[V] OPF reports both token- and span-level P/R/F1.** Span F1 sits consistently below token F1 (0.926 vs 0.960 on 300k; 0.617 vs 0.842 on CredData) because of boundary mismatches. This is a concrete illustration that the matching rule changes conclusions. — [PDF](https://cdn.openai.com/pdf/c66281ed-b638-456a-8ce1-97e9f5264a90/OpenAI-Privacy-Filter-Model-Card.pdf)
- **[K] Segura-Bedmar, I., Martínez, P., Herrero-Zazo, M. (2013). "SemEval-2013 Task 9: Extraction of Drug-Drug Interactions from Biomedical Texts (DDIExtraction 2013)." *SemEval 2013*, 341–350.** Defines the strict / exact-boundary / partial / type matching scheme (COR/INC/PAR/MIS/SPU). The `nervaluate` library implements it, and RECAP and many PII papers use it. — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/S13-2056/)
- **[K] Stubbs et al. (2015)** report i2b2 2014 under strict and relaxed matching, at token and entity level, and for HIPAA-only vs all PHI. This is the clinical precedent for reporting several matching regimes. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbi.2015.06.007)
- **[V] Mind the Gap (2026):** "aggregate benchmark scores obscure deployment-critical weaknesses". Relaxed span matching gives an optimistic upper bound for redaction. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03464)
- **[V] RedactionBench R-Score:** character-level, masking-style-invariant, separates contextual ambiguity from strict precision. Human agreement on contextual redaction was 47.7%, showing that gold labels for "contextual" PII are inherently uncertain. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18782)
- **[K] Pilán et al. (2022) TAB:** entity-level recall over all mentions (direct vs quasi identifiers) plus information-weighted precision; it argues that token-level F1 against NER categories misrepresents privacy protection. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1162/coli_a_00458)
- **[K] Lison et al. (2021):** argues disclosure risk (re-identification) rather than detection accuracy should be the target metric. — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2021.acl-long.323/)

### Inferences
- Tūmataiti's **document leakage rate** sits between TAB's entity-level recall (per entity, all mentions) and a "zero-leak document" criterion. Formally, it is a document-level generalisation of TAB's ER metric. Report it alongside TAB-style per-entity-type recall so readers can map it to prior work. Also consider reporting it at the OPF operating points, giving a leakage-vs-over-redaction curve.
- Because leakage is computed against gold spans, missing gold labels (as in 300k) make leakage look *better* than reality: unlabelled PII cannot "leak". The NZ synthetic set, where gold is generated, avoids this. This asymmetry should be stated as a threat to validity.
- Token-level F1 inflates performance relative to span F1. Tūmataiti's exact + partial span reporting matches best practice. Adding token-level numbers would allow direct comparison with OPF's card.

### Gaps
- No corrected or relabelled public release of PII-Masking-300k was found. OPF's corrections are described but not released (as far as found).
- No published study validates document-level leakage rate as a metric against actual re-identification outcomes.
- The full ai4privacy 200k audit details (tag counts, leakage) are attributed to arXiv:2504.12308 by a secondary summary only; verify.

## 5. Gaps: non-English / non-Western names, country-specific identifiers, fairness

### Takeaway
There is direct peer-reviewed evidence that PII/name detectors miss names associated with Black and Asian/Pacific Islander people more often (Mansfield et al. 2022), and that NER quality varies by name demographics (Mishra et al. 2020). The 2026 OPF evidence shows collapse on non-Latin scripts and weak name recall in general. Country-specific identifier coverage is ad hoc: Presidio covers about 20 countries but not New Zealand. No published work was found on te reo Māori or Pasifika names in PII detection, a clear gap Tūmataiti can occupy.

### Cited Findings
- **[V] Mansfield, C., Paullada, A., Howell, K. (2022). "Behind the Mask: Demographic bias in name detection for PII masking." *Proc. Second Workshop on Language Technology for Equality, Diversity and Inclusion (LTEDI 2022)*, 76–89. arXiv:2205.04505.** Tests three off-the-shelf PII maskers (commercial plus an open RoBERTa-based one) with customer-service templates and demographically associated names. All systems showed significant false-negative disparities, highest for names associated with Black and Asian/Pacific Islander individuals; the open RoBERTa model showed smaller disparities. Code: csmansfield/pii-masking-bias. This is the template for a fairness audit of Tūmataiti using Māori, Pasifika, Asian and Pākehā name lists. — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2022.ltedi-1.10); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.04505)
- **[K] Mishra, S., He, S., Belli, L. (2020). "Assessing Demographic Bias in Named Entity Recognition." arXiv:2008.03415 (preprint; AKBC 2020 Bias workshop).** Template-based evaluation with US census-derived names. NER models recognise names associated with some demographic groups (notably white names) more reliably than others. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.03415)
- **[V] OPF model card:** names "underrepresented in training data, or that follow conventions different from the dominant training distribution, may be more likely to be missed or inconsistently bounded". The model is "primarily English", with degradation on non-Latin scripts. — [PDF](https://cdn.openai.com/pdf/c66281ed-b638-456a-8ce1-97e9f5264a90/OpenAI-Privacy-Filter-Model-Card.pdf)
- **[V] Uppala (2026):** OPF Arabic F1 0.04 and Cyrillic 0.03. It flags "culturally variable entities" including person names (F1 0.40) and gives guidance on "which language families to avoid". — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02616)
- **[V] Singh & Narayanan (2025):** test sets built from India/UK/US identifier rules expose failures of popular HF PII models on country-specific formats and cross-lingual text. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.12308)
- **[V] Rajgarhia et al. (2025) RECAP:** 13 low-resource locales, where fine-tuned NER performs far worse than hybrid regex + LLM. This supports locale-specific deterministic rules. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.07551)
- **[V] Presidio supported entities:** country recognisers for the US, UK, Spain, Italy, Poland, Singapore, Australia, India, Finland, Korea, Nigeria, the Philippines, Canada, Sweden, South Africa, Thailand, Turkey and Germany; **no NZ**. — [Presidio docs](https://presidio.dataprivacystack.org/supported_entities/)
- **[K] Marimon et al. (2019) MEDDOCAN** shows the community building language-specific de-id corpora (Spanish) because English models do not transfer. — [CEUR-WS](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-2421/MEDDOCAN_overview.pdf)

### Inferences
- A Mansfield-style template audit across NZ name groups (Māori, including macronised vs unmacronised spellings, Samoan, Tongan, Chinese, Indian and Pākehā names), measuring per-group false-negative/leakage rates for OPF, GLiNER, Presidio and Tūmataiti, would be a novel and publishable contribution. Nothing equivalent was found for NZ.
- Macron handling (ā, ē, ī, ō, ū) and tokenisation of Māori names and place names (e.g., multi-word iwi/hapū names, which may count as affiliation) are plausible failure points for BPE-based teachers. This is untested in the literature found.
- Country-specific checksum validators are a well-defined and under-served niche: commercial and open tools cover other jurisdictions, but NZ IRD (mod-11) and NHI (old and new 2022+ formats) have no published detector evaluation.

### Gaps
- No academic study was found on PII detection or NER performance for te reo Māori, Pasifika names or NZ identifiers (IRD, NHI, NZ bank account formats). This should be confirmed with a targeted search of NZ venues (e.g., ALTA, NZ health informatics) before claiming absolute novelty.
- No published fairness evaluation of OPF or GLiNER-PII across demographic name groups was found.
- Peer-reviewed literature on fairness of PII detectors beyond names (e.g., addresses in non-Western formats) appears thin; none found in this pass.
