# Differentially Private and Federated Training of NLP / NER Models, and Memorisation Attacks

Scope: literature supporting Tūmataiti's plans to (a) DP-SGD fine-tune a ~100–150M-parameter PII-detector encoder and audit it with membership-inference / canary attacks; (b) federate improvement across 2–5 non-IID organisations/iwi with Flower, secure aggregation and site/user-level DP; (c) release DP usage statistics; plus Indigenous governance of federated models.

## Q1. DP-SGD foundations and DP fine-tuning of pretrained language models (incl. PEFT/LoRA, DP for NER/token classification, typical ε and utility costs)

### Takeaway
DP-SGD (per-example clipping + Gaussian noise + moments/RDP accounting) is the standard; since 2022 it is well established that fine-tuning a *pretrained* transformer under DP-SGD with ε≈3–8 (δ≈1/n or smaller) loses only a few points on GLUE-style tasks, and that parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA, adapters, bias-only) makes DP cheaper and often more accurate. Work on DP specifically for NER/token classification is sparse (a handful of workshop papers and clinical studies), so Tūmataiti's ε-vs-F1 curve for a PII tagger would be a genuine contribution.

### Cited Findings

**Foundations**
- Abadi, M., Chu, A., Goodfellow, I., McMahan, H. B., Mironov, I., Talwar, K., Zhang, L. (2016). "Deep Learning with Differential Privacy." *ACM CCS 2016*, pp. 308–318. DOI 10.1145/2976749.2978318; arXiv:1607.00133. Introduces DP-SGD (per-example gradient clipping to norm C, Gaussian noise σC, Poisson/uniform lot sampling) and the moments accountant, giving much tighter ε than strong composition. Baseline algorithm Opacus implements. **[well-known, details from prior knowledge]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1607.00133)
- Dwork, C., Roth, A. (2014). "The Algorithmic Foundations of Differential Privacy." *Foundations and Trends in Theoretical Computer Science* 9(3–4):211–407. DOI 10.1561/0400000042. Textbook reference for (ε,δ)-DP, Laplace/Gaussian mechanisms, composition. **[well-known]** — [Author PDF](https://www.cis.upenn.edu/~aaroth/Papers/privacybook.pdf)
- Mironov, I. (2017). "Rényi Differential Privacy." *IEEE CSF 2017*. arXiv:1702.07476. RDP accounting used by Opacus's default `rdp` accountant. **[well-known]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1702.07476)
- Gopi, S., Lee, Y. T., Wutschitz, L. (2021). "Numerical Composition of Differential Privacy." *NeurIPS 2021*. arXiv:2106.02848. PRV accountant (tighter than RDP; available in Opacus as `prv`). **[well-known]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.02848)
- Yousefpour, A., Shilov, I., Sablayrolles, A., et al. (2021). "Opacus: User-Friendly Differential Privacy Library in PyTorch." arXiv:2109.12298 **(preprint; NeurIPS 2021 PriML workshop)**. The library named in Tūmataiti's plan; vectorised per-sample gradients, RDP/PRV accountants. **[well-known]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.12298)
- Ponomareva, N., Hazimeh, H., Kurakin, A., Xu, Z., Denison, C., McMahan, H. B., Vassilvitskii, S., Chien, S., Thakurta, A. (2023). "How to DP-fy ML: A Practical Guide to Machine Learning with Differential Privacy." *Journal of Artificial Intelligence Research* 77. arXiv:2303.00654. Practitioner guide; proposes reporting tiers (roughly ε≤1 "strong", ε≤10 "reasonable", larger ε weak/empirical-only), recommends stating unit of privacy (example vs user), accountant, and δ≪1/n. **DP fine-tuning of pretrained LMs**
- Li, X., Tramèr, F., Liang, P., Hashimoto, T. (2022). "Large Language Models Can Be Strong Differentially Private Learners." *ICLR 2022*. arXiv:2110.05679. Full fine-tuning of BERT/RoBERTa/GPT-2 under DP-SGD at ε∈{3,8} gets close to non-private baselines when hyperparameters are tuned for DP (large batches, more epochs, aligned fine-tuning objective); introduces "ghost clipping" so per-example clipping costs roughly non-private memory. Key evidence that a ~125M encoder is DP-trainable. **[well-known, details from prior knowledge]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.05679)
- Yu, D., Naik, S., Backurs, A., Gopi, S., Inan, H. A., Kamath, G., Kulkarni, J., Lee, Y. T., Manoel, A., Wutschitz, L., Yekhanin, S., Zhang, H. (2022). "Differentially Private Fine-tuning of Language Models." *ICLR 2022*. arXiv:2110.06500. DP with parameter-efficient methods (LoRA, adapters, compacter); reports RoBERTa-Large MNLI ≈87.8% at ε≈6.7 vs ≈90.2% non-private, with far less memory, and DP-LoRA on GPT-2 for generation. Primary citation for DP-LoRA. **[well-known; utility numbers from memory of abstract — spot-check]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.06500)
- Hu, E. J., Shen, Y., Wallis, P., et al. (2022). "LoRA: Low-Rank Adaptation of Large Language Models." *ICLR 2022*. arXiv:2106.09685. The PEFT method; fewer trainable parameters lowers DP noise dimension. **[well-known]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.09685)

Bias-only DP fine-tuning matching SOTA DP accuracy with near non-private efficiency; summarised in the Hu et al. survey. **[existence confirmed via survey]** — [Survey arXiv:2301.09112](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.09112)
- Survey: Hu, L., et al. (2023). "Differentially Private Natural Language Models: Recent Advances and Future Directions." arXiv:2301.09112 **(preprint)**. Notes Wunderlich et al. - Anil, R., Ghazi, B., Gupta, V., Kumar, R., Manurangsi, P. (2022). "Large-Scale Differentially Private BERT." *Findings of EMNLP 2022*. arXiv:2108.01624. **DP for NER / token classification specifically**

Closest prior work to Tūmataiti's combined plan. **[verified existence/venue; method details not fetched — ACL page timed out]** — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2021.privatenlp-1.4)

- Diera, A., Lell, N., Garifullina, A., Scherp, A. (2022). Generated text from fine-tuned BERT on email/blog data; entity memorisation <10% in all setups; DP fine-tuning strongly reduces memorised entities at a downstream-performance cost. **[verified]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.03749v1)

Relevant as an encoder-specific (non-generative) leakage baseline. **[well-known, details from prior knowledge]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.07762)

### Inferences
- For a ~125M encoder (e.g. RoBERTa-base/DeBERTa-v3-small class) Tūmataiti should expect, from Li et al. and Yu et al., a few F1 points loss at ε≈3–8; rare entity classes (NZ-specific identifiers, te reo names) will likely suffer more than the average because DP clipping/noise disproportionately harms low-frequency classes (a known DP fairness effect — Bagdasaryan et al., NeurIPS 2019 "Differential Privacy Has Disparate Impact on Model Accuracy", arXiv:1905.12101 [well-known, not re-checked]).
- DP-LoRA or DP-BiTFiT on top of a non-privately pretrained encoder (pretrained on public text only) is the most defensible configuration; the public-pretraining assumption should be stated explicitly.
- Token classification has a subtlety: the DP unit is the *training example* (sentence/document), not the token; a single person's PII may span many examples, so example-level ε understates person-level risk unless contributions are bounded per person/document.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed benchmark found of DP-SGD fine-tuning of a BERT-class model for *PII/de-identification NER* (e.g. i2b2 2014) reporting an ε–F1 curve. The search for this returned only adjacent work (Hoory et al.; synthetic EHR via DP GPT-2).
- Jana & Biemann's quantitative results were not retrieved (ACL Anthology fetch failed).

## Q2. Memorisation and attacks (canaries/exposure, extraction, membership inference, PII leakage, empirical DP auditing)

### Takeaway
The audit toolkit Tūmataiti needs is mature: canary insertion with the exposure metric (Carlini 2019), likelihood-ratio membership inference (LiRA, Carlini 2022), PII-specific extraction/reconstruction/inference attacks (Lukas 2023), and empirical DP lower bounds — now achievable in a single training run (Steinke, Nasr, Jagielski 2023). There is also NER-specific evidence: black-box membership inference against spaCy NER redaction models reached ~70% AUC, and a vocabulary timing side-channel reached ~99% AUC (Ali et al. 2023).

### Cited Findings

**Canaries, extraction, memorisation**
- Carlini, N., Liu, C., Erlingsson, Ú., Kos, J., Song, D. (2019). "The Secret Sharer: Evaluating and Testing Unintended Memorization in Neural Networks." *USENIX Security 2019*, pp. 267–284. arXiv:1802.08232. Inserts random "canary" sequences (e.g. "my SSN is ___") and measures **exposure** = log2(|R|) − log2(rank of canary among candidates); shows memorisation arises early and that DP training eliminates measurable exposure. The template for Tūmataiti's canary test (insert synthetic NZ IRD/NHI numbers). **[well-known, details from prior knowledge]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.08232)
- Carlini, N., Tramèr, F., Wallace, E., Jagielski, M., Herbert-Voss, A., Lee, K., Roberts, A., Brown, T., Song, D., Erlingsson, Ú., Oprea, A., Raffel, C. (2021). "Extracting Training Data from Large Language Models." *USENIX Security 2021*. arXiv:2012.07805. Extracted hundreds of verbatim training sequences (incl. names, phone numbers, emails) from GPT-2 by sampling + membership ranking. **[well-known]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.07805)
- Carlini, N., Ippolito, D., Jagielski, M., Lee, K., Tramèr, F., Zhang, C. (2023). "Quantifying Memorization Across Neural Language Models." *ICLR 2023*. arXiv:2202.07646. Memorisation grows log-linearly with model size, duplication count and context length. **[well-known]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.07646)
- Lukas, N., Salem, A., Sim, R., Tople, S., Wutschitz, L., Zanella-Béguelin, S. (2023). "Analyzing Leakage of Personally Identifiable Information in Language Models." *IEEE S&P 2023*. arXiv:2302.00539. Taxonomy of PII leakage (extraction, reconstruction, inference); attacks on GPT-2 fine-tuned on case law, health care and email; argues scrubbing alone is a false assurance, and that DP reduces but does not eliminate PII leakage while scrubbing+DP trade off utility. Strongly relevant: it motivates PII detection *and* DP together. **[verified]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.00539v1); [MSR](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/?p=939993)
- Brown, H., Lee, K., Mireshghallah, F., Shokri, R., Tramèr, F. (2022). "What Does it Mean for a Language Model to Preserve Privacy?" *ACM FAccT 2022*. arXiv:2202.05520. Argues DP's example-level unit and data sanitisation both mismatch the contextual, multi-occurrence nature of secrets in text. Useful framing for the community-text setting. **[well-known]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.05520)

**Membership inference**
- Shokri, R., Stronati, M., Song, C., Shmatikov, V. (2017). "Membership Inference Attacks Against Machine Learning Models." *IEEE S&P 2017*, pp. 3–18. DOI 10.1109/SP.2017.41; arXiv:1610.05820. Shadow-model MIA. **[well-known]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.05820)
- Carlini, N., Chien, S., Nasr, M., Song, S., Terzis, A., Tramèr, F. (2022). "Membership Inference Attacks From First Principles." *IEEE S&P 2022*. arXiv:2112.03570. LiRA: per-example likelihood ratio using in/out shadow models; argues for reporting TPR at low FPR (e.g. 0.1%) rather than AUC. Tūmataiti should follow this reporting convention. **[well-known]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.03570)
- Mireshghallah, F., Goyal, K., Uniyal, A., Berg-Kirkpatrick, T., Shokri, R. (2022). "Quantifying Privacy Risks of Masked Language Models Using Membership Inference Attacks." *EMNLP 2022*. arXiv:2203.03929. Likelihood-ratio MIA against MLMs (clinical BERT) is far stronger than earlier loss-threshold attacks. Directly relevant to encoder models. **[well-known]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.03929)
- Ali, R. S., Zhao, B. Z. H., Asghar, H. J., Nguyen, T., Wood, I. D., Kaafar, M. A. (2023). "Unintended Memorization and Timing Attacks in Named Entity Recognition Models." *PoPETs* 2023(2):329–346. DOI 10.56553/popets-2023-0056; arXiv:2211.02245. Black-box NER-as-a-service for redaction (spaCy): memorisation-based membership attack reaches **70% AUC** on a text-redaction use case; a **timing side-channel** exploiting training-derived vocabularies reaches **99.23% AUC**. The most directly analogous threat model to Tūmataiti's PII detector; also implies inference latency should not depend on vocabulary membership. **[verified]** — [PoPETs](https://petsymposium.org/popets/2023/popets-2023-0056.php); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.02245v1)
- A NoDaLiDa 2023 study on a clinical BERT model (KTH-hosted PDF, "Using Membership Inference Attacks to Evaluate Privacy-Preserving…") found a SOTA MIA failed to detect the privacy benefit of pseudonymisation, suggesting MIAs may be inadequate for evaluating *token-level* PII protection. **[verified existence of PDF; full bibliographic details not retrieved]** — [KTH PDF](https://digitalfutures.kth.se/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/Using-Membership-Inference-Attacks-to-Evaluate-Privacy-Preserving.pdf)

**Auditing DP empirically**
- Jagielski, M., Ullman, J., Oprea, A. (2020). "Auditing Differentially Private Machine Learning: How Private is Private SGD?" *NeurIPS 2020*. arXiv:2006.07709. Uses poisoning canaries to derive empirical ε lower bounds. **[well-known]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.07709)
- Nasr, M., Song, S., Thakurta, A., Papernot, N., Carlini, N. (2021). "Adversary Instantiation: Lower Bounds for Differentially Private Machine Learning." *IEEE S&P 2021*. arXiv:2101.04535. Shows bounds are tight only for strong (gradient-access) adversaries; realistic black-box adversaries are far weaker than worst-case ε. **[well-known]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.04535)
- Nasr, M., Hayes, J., Steinke, T., Balle, B., Tramèr, F., Jagielski, M., Carlini, N., Terzis, A. (2023). "Tight Auditing of Differentially Private Machine Learning." *USENIX Security 2023*. arXiv:2302.07956. **[well-known, details from prior knowledge]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.07956)
- Steinke, T., Nasr, M., Jagielski, M. (2023). "Privacy Auditing with One (1) Training Run." *NeurIPS 2023* (Outstanding Paper). arXiv:2305.08846. Inserts/omits many canaries independently in a single run to obtain empirical ε lower bounds for DP-SGD; a third-party summary reports lower bounds of 0.7/1.2/1.8/3.5 for theoretical ε=1/2/4/8 on CIFAR-10 with 5,000 canaries (not verified against the paper). Makes auditing feasible on Tūmataiti's compute budget. **[verified]** — [NeurIPS proceedings](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/9a6f6e0d6781d1cb8689192408946d73-Abstract.html); [Liner summary](https://liner.com/review/privacy-auditing-with-one-1-training-run)

### Inferences
- Recommended audit battery: (1) canaries of NZ-format identifiers with exposure; (2) LiRA (or the one-run Steinke-style audit) reporting TPR@0.1%FPR; (3) a Lukas-style PII reconstruction test is less applicable to a non-generative tagger, but Ali et al.'s black-box confidence-based MIA and timing check are directly applicable.
- For an encoder classifier, "extraction" means recovering secrets via confidence scores/embeddings rather than text generation; the canary-exposure approach can be adapted by ranking candidate canary fillers by the tagger's confidence that they are a (memorised) entity.

### Gaps
- No published canary-exposure study specifically on a DP-trained token classifier for PII was found.
- Whether one-run auditing bounds are informative for large-vocabulary text models (vs CIFAR) remains to be checked in follow-up literature (2024–2026 work not searched in depth).

## Q3. Federated learning for NLP/NER: FedAvg, cross-silo with few non-IID clients, FedProx/personalisation, secure aggregation, DP-FedAvg/user-level DP, Flower

### Takeaway
FedAvg + DP-FedAvg (client-level clipping and noise) + secure aggregation is the canonical stack and is in production (Gboard, all neural LMs with DP guarantees, ρ-zCDP 0.2–2). But Tūmataiti's setting — 2–5 organisational *silos* — differs from cross-device FL: user/client-level DP over 2–5 clients is essentially meaningless (noise must hide a whole silo), so the literature on cross-silo FL recommends **record-level (or person-level within silo) DP applied locally at each silo** (ISRL-DP) plus personalisation to handle non-IID data. Federated NER specifically shows a consistent gap versus centralised training.

**Flower**
- Beutel, D. J., Topal, T., Mathur, A., Qiu, X., Fernandez-Marques, J., Gao, Y., Sani, L., Li, K. H., Parcollet, T., Porto Buarque de Gusmão, P., Lane, N. D. (2020). "Flower: A Friendly Federated Learning Research Framework." arXiv:2007.14390 **(preprint)**. Framework-agnostic FL; Flower provides SecAgg+ and DP wrappers (client-side and server-side fixed/adaptive clipping). **[well-known; author list from prior knowledge]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.14390)

### Inferences
- With 2–5 silos, use **local DP-SGD at each silo (ISRL-DP / record- or person-level within silo)** rather than DP-FedAvg client-level noise; client-level DP would require noise large enough to hide an entire iwi's contribution and would destroy utility. Secure aggregation with 2–5 parties offers limited protection (any n−1 colluding parties learn the remaining update) and SecAgg+ thresholds need care at such small n.
- Reviewer corrections are label-skewed and small; FedProx/SCAFFOLD for drift, and FedRep/Ditto-style personalisation (shared encoder, local heads or fine-tuned adapters) are the best-supported responses. DP-LoRA adapters are natural federated payloads (small, cheap to aggregate/encrypt).
- Expect a gap vs centralised training (FedNLP; Liu & Miller); report both.

### Gaps
- No federated NER study with only 2–5 silos *and* DP *and* human-correction (active-learning) signal was found. Federated active learning / learning from reviewer corrections was not searched in depth.
- Flower's own DP/SecAgg+ documentation was not fetched; feature claims are from prior knowledge.

## Q4. DP for aggregate analytics/telemetry, and Indigenous/community governance of federated models

### Takeaway
Telemetry DP is deployed at scale in local-DP form (Google RAPPOR, Apple, Microsoft Windows) and central/shuffled forms; for a small number of trusted-curator deployments, **central DP counts** (Laplace/Gaussian with bounded per-user contribution) give far better accuracy than local DP. On Indigenous governance, CARE and Māori Data Sovereignty principles are well established, and there is a small but real literature arguing federated learning can support Indigenous data sovereignty (Boscarino et al. 2022; a US tribal data repository using federated ML presented at NeurIPS 2025) — but no Māori/iwi federated-learning deployment was found.

### Inferences
- For (c), Tūmataiti operates as a trusted curator of its own usage logs, so **central DP with contribution bounding** (Wilson et al.) is preferable to local DP; if iwi do not wish to trust a central curator, per-site DP counts summed across sites (distributed DP, optionally with SecAgg) are the federated analogue. Repeated releases need a privacy-budget ledger (Ding et al. address repeated collection).
- FL aligns with CARE "Authority to control" and Te Mana Raraunga "Kaitiakitanga" only partially: model updates and the aggregated model are themselves derived data, so governance should cover who may receive the global model, per-iwi veto/withdrawal, and unlearning — the latter flagged as lacking clear mechanisms without retraining.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed Māori/iwi federated learning deployment or governance framework for federated models was found; nor any work mapping DP parameter choice (ε) to Indigenous collective-privacy notions. Collective/group privacy (as opposed to individual DP) is a recognised conceptual gap that Tūmataiti could address.
- Te Mana Raraunga principles page and Boscarino et al. full text were not fetched.
